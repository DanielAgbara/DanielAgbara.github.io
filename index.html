About Me

I am a Mechanical Engineering master’s student at the University of Texas at Austin, concentrating in Dynamic Systems and Control. My work combines robot kinematics, motion control, perception, and machine learning to develop autonomous systems that interact effectively with people and physical environments.

At UT Austin’s HERO Lab, I contribute to a robotic cleaning system for surgical environments. My work includes 6-DOF manipulation, body-tracking teleoperation, stereo-vision perception, object detection, camera–robot calibration, force sensing, and multimodal demonstration-data collection.

I previously earned a B.S. in Mechanical Engineering with a minor in Computer Science from Drexel University. I am interested in robotics, controls, autonomous systems, and research opportunities that translate rigorous engineering into meaningful real-world applications.

Planar 2R Robot Manipulator Simulation

Numerical Methods are great tools for Mechanical Engineering Design. This project involved developing a numerical simulation of a planar 2R robotic manipulator subjected to time-varying joint torques. The purpose was to model, solve, and visualize the dynamic response of the manipulator using multiple numerical integration techniques, while analyzing motion, energy, and power consumption over time. Click for more information.

Knight Trials

Knight Trials is a collaborative game development project created by a team of three students at Drexel University. Designed to challenge players’ strategy and reflexes, the game immerses them in a medieval fantasy world where they must prove themselves as worthy defenders of the Realm. Players journey through three uniquely designed elemental mazes—Earth, Ice, and Mud—each presenting distinct terrain mechanics, environmental hazards, and dynamic enemy encounters. Closed-Loop Hydroponic Planter

As part of a senior design project for MEM 459, my team and I developed an automated hydroponic irrigation system using an Arduino-based closed-loop PID controller.
